Hi! I’m Xi Chen (陈希), a second-year M.S.E. student in Computer Science at Johns Hopkins University. I am fortunate to be advised by Prof. Laixi Shi! Before JHU, I received my B.Eng. in Computer Science from Nankai University.

My research aims to build agents that make reliable decisions in real-world tasks. I work along two lines:

  1. Robust Optimization — Robust reinforcement learning and generative modeling for reliable decision-making under uncertainty and distribution shifts.
  2. Systems for Self-Evolving Agents — Adaptive and autonomous learning systems for multi-agent intelligence and robotics, with robot policies that transfer from simulation to the real world.

I am applying for a Ph.D. to pursue this agenda, and I’m always happy to connect about research or collaborations.

selected research projects

Leveraging Generative Uncertainty Modeling for Distributionally Robust Offline Reinforcement Learning   · Apr 2026 – Present
Johns Hopkins University — with Prof. Laixi Shi, Prof. Jianyi Yang and Jiaqi Wen · Under review

  • A non-rectangular, trajectory-level uncertainty set built from a conditional flow model, with the flow-matching loss as a tractable discrepancy that implies a Wasserstein bound.
  • Implemented adversarial fine-tuning of the flow world model within this set; its adverse multi-step trajectories augment conservative offline value learning on IQL, CQL and BCQ.
  • Best OOD score on six MuJoCo task–dataset settings (+6.7 normalized points over the strongest baseline), with gains on an EPANET water-network case study.

Track-Any-Point Foundation Model for Endoscopic Scenes   · Feb 2026 – Present
ARCADE Lab, Johns Hopkins University — with Dr. Lalithkumar Seenivasan · Manuscript in preparation

  • Extended AllTracker with dual correlation (long-range anchors plus flow-aligned adjacent frames) for bronchoscopy, reaching 91.9% δavg, above the anchor-only baseline.
  • Added a cosine-gated feature memory that cuts invisible-point loss by ~9%, and sliding-window inference with feature reuse at ~120–156 fps.

Backdoor Attacks on Multivariate Time Series Forecasting with Missing Values   · Dec 2024 – May 2025
Undergraduate thesis, DBIS Lab, Nankai University — with Dr. Xiangrui Cai

  • A backdoor attack for forecasting under missing values, where prior attacks assume complete inputs and struggle to be both effective and stealthy.
  • Critical-time-step-guided imputation and dynamic trigger placement give 17–43% relative improvement over baseline attacks on PeMS traffic data.
